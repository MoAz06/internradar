## Process, as the company describes it

- To apply you need “an updated CV or resume and a current unofficial or official transcript in English”.
- Before interviews “you may be asked to take the Google Hiring Assessment”, and maybe a role-related assessment “like a coding exercise”.
- Then “one or two shorter conversations over phone or video with your recruiter”.
- Interviews are “a panel of interviews, either over video or in person with a rotating cast of Googlers”. No brain teasers, and “AI tools are not permitted to be used during your interviews.”
- Once interviews start the process takes “roughly 6-8 weeks”.
- For the internships, “The specific team and project(s) will be determined based on your educational background, interest and skills.”
- Student Researcher applicants are considered for projects across Google research teams, and hearing back “can take upwards of 90+ days”.
- You can apply to “up to three jobs within a rolling 30 day window”.

## Re-apply rule

Google asks people who applied or interviewed and were not selected to “wait at least a year to gain additional experience before reapplying for the same type of role”. You can apply to at most three jobs in a rolling 30-day window.

## Pay, relocation, visa

- Pay for Zurich: not stated. The Student Researcher posting lists yearly rates for Germany, France, Sweden and the Netherlands only (Germany, Master's: “€71000 (EUR) + 0% bonus target”).
- Relocation: not mentioned.
- Visa: the Software Engineering and Research Scientist PhD internships say applicants “will not be eligible for immigration sponsorship”. The Student Researcher posting says you must hold valid work authorization or meet local immigration rules. The Data Science posting does not mention visas.
